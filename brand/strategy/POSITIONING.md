# Brand positioning and audience

Binyled’s Storyscape · Strategy brief · November 12, 2026

## Recommended position

**Own the evening the host gets to play in.** Binyled’s Storyscape is a private tabletop roleplaying experience for hosts who want to bring friends together and play alongside them. A game master prepares a self-contained adventure around the group, introduces the guests to play, and guides the evening at their table.

| Brand decision | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Public brand | **Binyled’s Storyscape**. Use the full name on a first encounter. Use **Storyscape** in running copy once the full name is established. |
| Flagship | **The Private Storyscape**, a single-evening commission concept. A first adventure, a personal occasion, and a possible next chapter are conversation starters. |
| Category | **Private in-home tabletop adventures.** Expand to “A guided tabletop roleplaying evening for your group” when the audience may hear “tabletop” and think only of board games. |
| Promise | **You host the people. I host the world.** Pair it with the literal category on first contact. |
| Signature | **I build the world. You shape the story.** Use where the audience already knows what the experience is. |
| Functional value | The practitioner shapes the story, prepares an opening, guides guests through decisions and rules, and keeps the session moving. Confirm all inclusions in the agreed scope. |
| Emotional value | The host can sit with their friends and take part. New guests have a way into the story, and experienced guests have room to improvise. |
| Geography | Bentonville and Northwest Arkansas are the intended service area. Confirm the date, location, and travel fit individually if the service opens. |
| First action | **Explore the experience** for the exemplar. **Request a private consultation** for a live service with an accepted intake path. |

The word “premium” is an internal ambition for the quality of preparation. Public copy should make that preparation tangible through a sample guest introduction, the host checklist, or an example of the first meaningful choice. Use specific, welcoming language a host could forward to friends.

## Who makes the decision

| Audience | When they look | What they need to see | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary · Host with a group in mind** | A birthday, reunion, or evening with friends needs a focal activity. The host enjoys gathering people and wants to join in. | A plain description of play, what the practitioner handles, and what the host needs to prepare. | Read the host guide, then bring the group, occasion, and a possible date to consultation when available. |
| **Secondary · Regular player or usual game master** | The regular facilitator wants a seat as a player or wants to bring friends with varied experience together. | The group makes meaningful choices. Familiarity, tone, and game system are discussed, and the session has a defined ending. | Read the worked example, then discuss the table’s play preferences. |
| **Influencer · First-time guest** | The host forwards the invitation. The guest wonders whether they need rules knowledge, acting skills, or equipment. | A concise player introduction, an example of a choice, and a clear way to ask for a pause or different participation format. | Read or hear the player introduction and share practical preferences privately. |
| **Potential referrer · A person who helps hosts plan gatherings** | Someone asks for a private activity built around a specific group. | A forwardable description with service area, typical fit, and what a consultation does. | Pass along an owner-approved host guide or service link. |

Prioritize the host with a known group. Open with the occasion and the desired experience. Treat demographics, purchasing power, and referral propensity as untested. Large drop-in events, background entertainment, and open-table seating require different operating models.

## Message order

1. **Benefit** — Play at your own gathering.
2. **Offer** — A private tabletop roleplaying adventure, prepared around your group and guided in your home.
3. **How it works** — Talk through the group and tone, agree the scope, then play a self-contained story with rules guidance as it’s needed.
4. **Demonstration** — Show a concrete opening choice from the fictional Lantern Bay example. A lighthouse shines inland and reveals a gate in a stone wall. Players can visit the lighthouse, ask witnesses at the market, or take the path beyond the gate. [Adventure opening](../../examples/lantern-bay/05-adventure.md#opening-read-or-paraphrase)
5. **Invitation** — Give the host one clear next action for the current operating mode.

### Lead copy for a service page

> You host the people. I host the world.
>
> A private tabletop adventure shaped around your guests. I prepare the story, help everyone get started, and guide the evening so you can play alongside your friends.

Use the service-page copy once the owner chooses to invite inquiries. For a public exemplar, use “Explore how a private tabletop adventure could be planned, prepared, and guided around a group.”

## Brand architecture

| Name or asset | Role | Where it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Binyled’s Storyscape** | Public-facing identity of the in-home tabletop concept. | Storyscape pages, approved collateral, and correspondence for the approved operating mode. |
| **The Private Storyscape** | Flagship offer name under the Storyscape identity. | Experience page, host guide, and scoped proposal. Use the parent brand’s visual identity. |
| **The Last Light of Lantern Bay** | Fictional example adventure and worked session. | Samples, player introduction, and annotated walkthrough. Label it as illustrative wherever audiences could mistake it for a booking or case study. |
| **Wayfarer Table** | Separate fictional remote-play profile showing the modular site in a different setting. | Technical demonstrations and the remote example. Use its own identity and a visible fictional-example label. |
| **Modular Hugo master site** | Internal reusable implementation and composition workflow. | Repository docs and exemplar materials for builders. The host journey stays focused on the gathering. |

## Evidence and claims

| Material | What it supports | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Host guide, sample opening, and Lantern Bay workflow | Concrete examples of how preparation, onboarding, choices, and follow-up are designed. | Describe the visible method. Keep the synthetic label with the sample. |
| Scene artwork and styled collateral | Art direction and a view of the intended atmosphere. | Don’t present a scene as documentation of a real event or client. |
| Consultation and local intake source | A designed inquiry flow with documented acceptance work outstanding. | In demo materials, label the flow as an example. Live wording follows real intake acceptance and owner approval. |
| Playtest or actual client evidence | Outcome claims tied to the specific session and what was observed. | Obtain the applicable permission for public photos, quotes, and attribution. Keep practice-session findings labeled as such. |
| Capacity, scope, and operator facts | Dates, travel fit, group size, duration, materials, fees, and terms. | Confirm with the owner and put agreed details in the written scope. Leave unconfirmed facts out of published assets. |
