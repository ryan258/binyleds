# Storyscape campaign and launch playbook

November 2026 · A bounded plan the owner can start, stop, or resume

## Campaign brief

**Creative idea** — You host the people. I host the world.  
**Who it is for** — A Northwest Arkansas host planning an evening with friends or family who wants to play alongside the group. The first message assumes a mix of experience and starts with what the host needs to know.  
**First asset** — The [host’s checklist](../../content/host-guide/index.md), offered as a practical planning aid. Pair it with the [host one-sheet](../collateral/host-one-sheet.pdf) for direct sharing or an approved partner introduction.  
**Desired action** — Read the checklist. With public intake open, ask about an actual gathering when ready.  
**Creative treatment** — Use one close view of a prepared table and a short line in live type. Use the first-choice example from [messaging](MESSAGING.md) when a reader needs to understand how play works. Give the image a visible concept label where applicable.

## Choose the release mode

| Mode | Shareable destination | Ask | What success looks like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Private working kit | Local exemplar, one-sheet, and Lantern Bay pack | Review one artifact or try the fictional opening scene. | Ryan can reuse the material and resume easily. A reviewer can describe the experience in a sentence. |
| Public portfolio | Owner-approved exemplar page or excerpt with example status up front | Explore the exemplar or read the host guide. | A reader understands the site and adventure are examples. Any public reuse rights are clear. |
| Active local service | Approved public landing page, host guide, and verified inquiry path | Request a private consultation. | A host can make an informed inquiry and receive a clear fit decision within the owner’s agreed capacity. |

To open a service campaign, resolve the owner decisions and acceptance evidence in the [readiness ledger](../../docs/readiness.md). Confirm the service area and capacity, pricing method and commercial terms, public contact, image-use approvals, and the route a real inquiry takes from submission to receipt to operator reply. Use the [operations guide](../../docs/site-operations.md) for deployment and provider acceptance. Confirm that the privacy notice describes actual practice. This kit prepares materials for review and includes no publication or outreach schedule.

## Bounded pilot for the service mode

Use one audience, one asset, and one channel the owner already uses. Choose a cap the owner can comfortably respond to, and record a review date. A single small cycle can fit into two to four weeks or stop sooner when there is enough evidence to make a decision.

| Step | What to do | What to learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Check comprehension | Show the one-sheet and checklist privately to a willing reader who is unfamiliar with tabletop play. Ask them to describe what guests do and whether an inquiry reserves a date. | Can a host explain the evening to someone else? |
| 2. Test one route | Share the checklist through one approved path below, after verifying it opens and the next step works. | Are the right people reaching the checklist and asking relevant questions? |
| 3. Handle responses | Reply personally using the [consultation templates](CONSULTATION.md). Record broad fit outcomes, time spent, and the one question that repeats. | Can the owner sustain preparation and correspondence alongside any real sessions? |
| 4. Decide | Continue at the same scale, revise the message or route, or close the trial. Add a second channel only if the first is understood and manageable. | Which action follows from the actual responses and workload? |

## Copy for a warm introduction

**Subject** — A checklist for planning a private game night

> Hi [name], you mentioned bringing [group or occasion] together. The checklist at [host guide URL] covers what guests need to know and what’s worth agreeing ahead of a private tabletop evening. If you’d like to talk through your gathering, you can tell me the rough guest count and a possible date through [approved consultation URL].

For a public portfolio, close with “The guide is part of a tabletop service-site example. I’d welcome a short note on whether it answers a host’s first questions.” Offer it as a direct reply to an existing conversation. An introduction is a draft for owner review and manual sending.

## Lightweight measurement

Use an offline note with weekly or per-cycle totals. Log owner effort with the same care as inquiry counts. At small volumes record raw counts and examples of recurring questions with identifying details removed. Use a real denominator before reporting a percentage. An unmeasured impression count stays unknown.

| Field | Definition | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Window and variant | Dates and one short identifier, e.g. `host-guide-a`. | The pilot note. |
| Approved placements | Count of times the asset was posted or shared, by broad channel. | Manual tally. |
| Useful responses | A relevant question, request for an introduction, or consultation request. | Manual tally from conversations. |
| New inquiries | Unique requests confirmed received. A retry of the same request counts once. | Operator inbox or verified correspondence. |
| Practical fits | Inquiry whose expected date, location, format, and requirements can proceed to scope discussion. | Manual aggregate after private review. |
| Outcomes | Scope offered, agreed, declined, or still open. | Aggregate, without private consultation contents. |
| Owner effort | Minutes on making, sharing, responding, and preparing, plus a brief capacity note. | Owner’s note. |
| Learning | One recurring question or friction point and the next change. | De-identified observation. |

You can dictate an entry in this format. “Host guide A. One introduction. Two replies. One inquiry, one fit. Twenty minutes. Asked whether beginners can play. Keep.” The numbers illustrate the format. Record A to continue at this scale, B to revise one thing, or C to stop.

If tracking is later selected, use campaign-level codes such as `utm_campaign=private-storyscape-pilot` and `utm_content=host-guide-a`, with `utm_source` naming the channel. Keep names, contact details, addresses, request IDs, and comfort or access notes out of URLs, marketing tools, and reports. Keep inquiry records in the approved private workflow. Use a de-identified tally for reporting.
