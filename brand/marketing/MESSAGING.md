# Storyscape messaging and copy guide

November 2026 · Working copy for Binyled’s Storyscape

## Verbal identity

**Name** — Binyled’s Storyscape  
**Service** — The Private Storyscape  
**Category** — Private in-home tabletop adventures  
**Signature line** — You host the people. I host the world.  
**Core promise** — You get to sit down with your guests while I prepare the adventure and guide the table.  
**Public service invitation, once open** — Request a private consultation

The default Storyscape build renders “Preview the consultation” on the main
consultation links and labels the disabled form as a demonstration. The `client`
profile changes which pages ship. The intake backend determines whether the form
accepts requests. [Consultation label template](../../layouts/_partials/consultation-link-label.html) · [Site operations](../../docs/site-operations.md)

Use the brand name at first mention, then Storyscape. Use the full service name in a proposal or service heading. In ordinary sentences, “a private adventure” or “your evening” often reads more naturally. “Game master” is the plain-language role, and “tabletop roleplaying” gives people a familiar category to search. Use “tabletop adventure” up front when the audience may be new to the hobby.

Use Bentonville and Northwest Arkansas when the service area is confirmed. Keep exact travel and dates subject to consultation. Use the singular practitioner voice for first-person copy and the brand name in directory or editorial copy. Refer to Binyleds as the repository and Wayfarer Table as the fictional remote example in technical materials. Introduce them only where the project itself is the subject.

## How the voice works

The existing voice blend is 40% gracious private host, 35% storyteller, and 25% discreet concierge. Let hospitality set the subject, the storyteller offer one specific image, and the concierge give the next step. In receipts, logistics, and privacy copy, use short, literal sentences. See the [production bible](../PRODUCTION_BIBLE.md) and [source vocabulary](../../data/brand.yaml).

| Job | Copy pattern | Ready-to-adapt line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explain what a host gains | Put the host at the table, then describe what the practitioner handles. | “Invite your favorite people and take a seat with them. I prepare the story and guide the evening.” |
| Welcome new players | Tell guests how play begins and how they can participate. | “I’ll introduce the opening scene, ask what you’d like to do, and explain the rules as they come up.” |
| Make customization concrete | Name one or two choices the host can bring. | “Tell me what brings the group together and whether they’d enjoy a warm mystery or a more dramatic adventure.” |
| Show imagination | Give the guests a decision inside a scene. | “By the time the lighthouse goes dark, someone at your table will have a theory about why.” |
| Explain the inquiry | Say what can be approximate and when a commitment happens. | “An approximate guest count and a possible date are enough to begin. I’ll confirm fit, scope, and price with you before you decide.” |
| Discuss access | Ask what would help, in practical terms. | “If a break, clearer print, or a different way to take a turn would help someone participate, we can discuss that privately.” |

For an event planner or potential referrer, lead with the fit and how to make an introduction. For hobbyists, name the opportunity to give a usual game master a seat as a player. For a first-time host, explain the experience in terms they can pass along to guests. Use sentence case, literal calls to action, and short paragraphs. Write in concrete nouns and verbs. Reserve ornament for an adventure title or a single line of atmosphere.

## Public service copy bank

Use these drafts after the service scope, destination, and contact path are confirmed. The proposed public category and place names match the current exemplar.

**One line**

> A private tabletop adventure for your guests, prepared and guided at your home in Northwest Arkansas.

**Short introduction**

> You host the people. I host the world. I shape a tabletop adventure around your guests, guide the story, and explain the rules as they come up. You get to play alongside the people you invited. A private consultation is the first step toward planning an evening in Northwest Arkansas.

**Homepage or flyer**

> **You host the people. I host the world.**
>
> I prepare and guide private in-home tabletop adventures for gatherings in Bentonville and Northwest Arkansas. Tell me who you’re bringing together and how you’d like the evening to feel. I’ll help you find the right starting point, whether this is everyone’s first game or your usual game master would like to play.
>
> Request a private consultation

**How it works**

1. **Tell me about the gathering.** A possible date, the general location, and a rough guest count are enough to get the conversation started.
2. **Agree on the evening.** I’ll confirm fit, timing, materials, scope, and the quote before you decide.
3. **Take a seat.** I prepare the agreed adventure and guide the story as your guests decide what happens.

**Common questions for cards, replies, or captions**

| Question | Short answer |
| --- | --- |
| What will guests actually do? | I describe a scene and invite your guests to decide how their characters respond. Their choices shape what happens next. |
| Can we all be new? | Yes. I explain how to make the first choice and introduce rules as the table needs them. |
| How many guests and how much time? | Tell me your rough guest count and timing. I’ll confirm what suits the group, room, and kind of adventure before we agree. |
| What comes with it? | The proposed scope spells out the preparation, materials, and guidance included in your evening. |
| Does a request hold the date? | A request opens a conversation. We confirm the date, scope, and price together before you decide. |

**Short bio for a service profile**

> Private in-home tabletop adventures in Bentonville and Northwest Arkansas. I prepare the story and guide the table so you can play with your guests.

## Social and editorial copy

**A host gets a seat** · Use with the main campaign card or a small table detail.

> If you usually do the explaining, it can be hard to play alongside your guests. For a private Storyscape, I prepare the adventure and guide the story. Your job is to invite the people you want at the table. See how the evening works.

**Everyone starts somewhere** · Pair with a guide or a simple rules-free example.

> Imagine the keeper of a lighthouse has gone missing. Do you climb the tower, talk to the neighbors, or follow the footprints down to the shore? A first tabletop adventure can begin with a choice that simple. See what to tell guests before game night.

**What to bring to the conversation** · Pair with a checklist graphic.

> Start with who’s coming, a date you have in mind, and the general location. The rest of your private tabletop evening can take shape together. Read the host’s checklist.

**Atmosphere caption**

> A prepared tabletop, ready for the first choice.

Describe the visible image in alt text and carry the message and action in the caption or adjacent text. When an image repeats nearby copy and adds no meaning, mark it decorative where the channel allows.

## Claim review

| Subject | Copy you can substantiate today | What supports a stronger claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adventure design | “Illustrative adventure concept” or “fictional worked example.” | Actual delivery or playtest record with approval to share the specific observations. |
| Service delivery | “Designed for private in-home tabletop gatherings” in portfolio mode. | Owner confirmation of actual service, area, intake, and operating terms. |
| Credentials and biography | An approved account of the approach in first person. | Owner-approved factual biography and evidence for any named credential. |
| Materials and access | Discuss options and practical needs in context. | Confirmed materials and arrangements for the actual scope, with specific acceptance evidence for any accessibility claim. |
| Photos, quotes, or partners | Labeled concepts and original excerpts from examples, subject to reuse approval. | Asset clearance or explicit approval of the specific client material, intended use, and attribution. |

Confirm the CTA goes to the intended destination, then read the headline and first paragraph aloud. A new reader should know what the evening is, what they do, and what happens when they respond.
