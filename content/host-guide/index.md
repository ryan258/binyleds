## Start with your people

Make a rough guest list and note whether the group is new to roleplaying,
experienced, or a mixture. Share what brings these people together and what
sort of evening they tend to enjoy. An approximate number is enough for an
initial inquiry; suitability is confirmed during consultation.

## Describe the evening

A birthday, reunion, or ordinary night with friends can each suggest a different
pace. Bring a preferred date or a few possibilities, plus any timing constraints.
Dates and duration remain subject to direct agreement.

Think in terms of atmosphere: playful, curious, mysterious, or dramatic. You do
not need a plot. A sense of what the group would enjoy is more useful.

## Consider the room

Be ready to describe the intended location, seating, table space, lighting, and
anything that may affect hearing or moving around the room. Setup needs, travel,
and materials are agreed once the format is understood.

Avoid buying equipment or rearranging the house before discussing what is
actually needed.

## Make comfort part of the plan

Ask guests privately about any themes they would prefer to avoid and practical
arrangements that would help them participate. Share only what is useful and
what they are comfortable having passed along. A diagnosis or personal history
is not required to discuss an access need.

Bring questions about breaks, explanations, and how guests can pause or raise
a concern during play.

## Leave with clear expectations

Before committing, confirm:

- The date, location, timing, and agreed group size.
- The story approach and game system, if a particular one matters to you.
- What preparation, materials, and on-the-night guidance are included.
- The price, any travel costs, and applicable payment and cancellation terms.
- What you and your guests need to do beforehand.

An inquiry starts the conversation; it does not reserve a date.
