## A commission, not a package

The Private Storyscape begins with people, not a prewritten module. The occasion,
personalities, experience levels, interests, tone, and comfort of the group give
the evening its shape.

That makes the experience closer to commissioning a private storyteller or
thoughtful hosted dinner than hiring someone to run a generic game. The details
exist in service of one outcome: your guests have a remarkable evening, and you
get to remain part of it.

## What may be shaped around your table

Every final scope is confirmed in the consultation. Depending on the people and
the story, that can include:

- A focused adventure created for the group
- Curated characters or guided character creation
- A welcoming introduction for first-time players
- Rules guidance that stays in the background
- Narrative pacing and attentive table facilitation
- Maps, music, miniatures, and selected physical details where useful
- A clear opening, satisfying arc, and room for the unexpected
- A possible next chapter when the table wants to continue

These are ingredients, not a checklist designed to make the evening look busy.
If a map, miniature, or prop does not help the guests enter the story, it does
not need to be there.

## What the host no longer has to carry

A meaningful tabletop evening usually asks one person to do several jobs at
once: prepare the story, explain the rules, keep the pace, read the room, involve
the quiet guest, and still somehow enjoy their own gathering.

The Storyscape moves that burden away from the host. You still set the spirit of
the evening and know your people best. You simply do not have to run the world
while everyone else plays in it.

## What guests experience

Guests are welcomed into the same story without being expected to arrive with
the same knowledge. Experienced players have space to take bold, surprising
directions. New players receive just enough context to make a meaningful choice
without sitting through a lecture.

The aim is not for everyone to admire a performance. It is for the table to
notice what they created together.

A quieter guest who came in unsure of the format may find the story has quietly
made room for them, a decision that only makes sense in their hands, arriving
right when they are ready to take it. That is not a script. It is what
attentive pacing is for.

## What this experience is not

- A public event or convention session
- A rules competition
- A children’s party performance
- A costume requirement
- A generic adventure with names swapped in
- A promise that every possible prop or production element will appear

The precise format, practical fit, and included materials are agreed before an
evening is confirmed.
