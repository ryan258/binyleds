## 1. Begin with a private consultation

The first conversation is about the gathering rather than game mechanics. Who
are you inviting? What brings them together? What do they enjoy? Which guests
have played before, and which may be entirely new?

This is also where practical fit is established: the preferred date, location,
expected guest count, tone, comfort boundaries, accessibility needs, and any
details that would make the evening feel distinctly yours.

If it helps to start somewhere, a few directions guests often react to:

- **The First-Timer's Journey** — light mechanics, high narrative, and a
  relaxed atmosphere for a table that is brand new to the hobby.
- **The Puzzle Box** — built for guests who love escape rooms, logic puzzles,
  and figuring things out together.
- **The Epic Continuation** — deeper lore and tactical stakes for a table of
  experienced players.

None of these are fixed packages. Naming one is optional — it only gives the
consultation somewhere to start.

The consultation is discovery, not a disguised checkout. A request does not
reserve a date or create a payment obligation.

## 2. Shape the story around the group

Once the evening is a fit, the creative work begins. The adventure, characters,
encounters, and supporting materials are selected or created to give this group
the right kind of choices.

Preparation follows three questions:

1. What will help every guest enter quickly?
2. What will give this particular group something to care about?
3. What can be removed so the evening feels natural rather than overproduced?

You receive the confirmed scope and practical expectations before the date is
finalized. Nothing important should be left to a surprise invoice or an
unspoken assumption.

## 3. Welcome the world into your home

On the evening, the table is introduced to the world with a light hand. Guests
learn by making choices. Rules are explained when they become useful. The pace
is guided, the room is read, and attention moves so no one becomes a spectator
in someone else's story.

You remain the host in the way that matters: you know the people, shape the
welcome, and sit down with them. The story and facilitation no longer rest on
your shoulders.

## 4. Let the final decision belong to the table

A Storyscape is prepared, but it is not predetermined. The evening reaches its
ending through what the guests choose together. Sometimes that ending also
opens a conversation about keepsakes, a follow-up chapter, or another private
gathering.

{{< callout title="The operating principle" tone="signature" >}}
Prepare the conditions carefully. Leave the story alive.
{{< /callout >}}

## What happens after a request

- The gathering details are reviewed for date, location, and experience fit.
- Any missing practical questions are handled directly.
- If the evening is a fit, the proposed shape and scope are confirmed.
- Only then are final scheduling and commercial details agreed.

If the event is not a fit, the answer should be clear and respectful. Honest fit
is part of the hospitality.
