## For the host who wants to be present

This experience is for someone who enjoys bringing people together but does not
want to spend weeks writing, teaching, organizing, and performing the entire
night. You care about the details because you care about the guests, and you
would also like to sit down with them.

The right host values originality, reliability, social awareness, and an
evening that feels personal without becoming showy.

## For mixed-experience groups

Some guests may know every convention of tabletop roleplaying. Others may not
know what a character sheet is. That difference is not a problem to solve before
the evening. It is part of the table the experience is designed to welcome.

New players receive:

- A clear reason to care about the opening situation
- Only the rules needed for the choice in front of them
- Characters or guidance that reduce blank-page pressure
- Time to ask without feeling behind

Experienced players receive:

- Enough depth to investigate, improvise, and take risks
- A story responsive to the table rather than locked to a script
- Room to help the group without becoming an unofficial second facilitator

{{< callout title="No homework required" tone="reassurance" >}}
Curiosity is the only prerequisite. Within the first scene, the evening should
belong to everyone.
{{< /callout >}}

## Occasions that can fit naturally

- A birthday centered on time together rather than spectacle
- A reunion or visiting group of close friends
- A thoughtful alternative to the usual dinner or game night
- A milestone for someone who loves stories and shared play
- An ordinary evening made intentionally memorable

The occasion does not need a theme. The people are enough to begin.

## When another format may be better

The Private Storyscape may not be the right fit for a large public event, a
drop-in crowd, a rules tournament, a children’s entertainment booking, or a
gathering where the game is expected to remain background noise.

That does not make the idea wrong. It simply means a different format may serve
the guests better. The consultation exists partly to make that distinction
before anyone overcommits.

## The questions that matter most

You do not need to arrive with a plot. It is more useful to know:

- Who is gathering?
- What do they enjoy together?
- What tone would feel comfortable?
- Is anyone likely to need more guidance, quieter participation, or another
  accommodation?
- What would make you say, the next morning, “That felt like us”?
