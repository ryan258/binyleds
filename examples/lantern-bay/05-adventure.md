# The Last Light of Lantern Bay

A system-neutral session for a group that wants discovery and a meaningful shared
choice. This is an unplaytested original example. Use the agreed game system if
the group has one; the resolution procedure below also works without dice.

## Opening, read or paraphrase

“The last market stalls are closing when the lighthouse turns away from the sea.
Its beam passes over your feet and stops at the old stone wall above town. A gate
appears where the wall was solid. Beyond it, pale fruit hangs in a quiet orchard.
The lighthouse bell rings once. Then someone in the crowd says, ‘That gate was
in my grandmother’s stories.’”

Offer the players three routes: the lighthouse, the market witnesses, or the
orchard path. They can choose another approach. Ask each willing player one
small question: what catches your attention? Passing is welcome.

## The truth the guide knows

Generations ago, the town promised to keep the orchard a shared refuge. In return,
its trees stored sunlight for nights when the lighthouse failed. The agreement
was renewed by an annual meal at the gate. Over time, people forgot; the path
faded, and the stored light dwindled.

Keeper Iona discovered the original agreement in a maintenance book. She turned
the beam inland so the orchard could be found. She has not stolen anything or
summoned a monster. The orchard’s caretaker, Pell, can release one last night of
light. After that, the townspeople must choose whether to renew a shared duty,
release the old agreement, or negotiate a smaller promise they can actually keep.

The weather will be inconvenient if the lamp goes dark, but no offstage life is
secretly at stake. State the stakes accurately. The point is a fair decision,
not a hidden punishment for choosing differently from the guide.

## People with understandable motives

| Person | Wants | Worries about | Helpful fact | How to play them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Iona, lighthouse keeper | A reliable light without keeping the orchard secret | Being blamed for turning the beam | The old meal renewed the promise | Direct, tired, ready to explain |
| Sef, market organizer | A refuge everyone can use | A new obligation falling on the same few neighbors | Several families remember pieces of the tradition | Practical; asks who will do the work |
| Pell, orchard caretaker | A promise people intend to keep | Another grand vow followed by absence | A smaller honest agreement can sustain a smaller light | Curious, literal, willing to negotiate |

No NPC requires persuasion mechanics to reveal a critical clue. Good questions,
helpful actions, or a reasonable request can earn context. If the group distrusts
someone, they can check another source.

## Clue map: no single bottleneck

| Necessary understanding | First route | Second route | If both are missed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The orchard and lighthouse are linked | Maintenance book at lighthouse | Fruit brightens when the beam touches it | Pell explains the exchange |
| The promise was communal care, not ownership | Sef’s remembered meal | Engraved invitation on gate | Iona reads the old agreement aloud |
| A smaller agreement is possible | Pell answers a question about fairness | Later amendments in the book | Sef proposes a modest trial |

Give the observable clue automatically when characters examine the relevant
place. Rolls, if your chosen system uses them, may decide speed, extra detail,
or a complication. They never decide whether the whole adventure can continue.

## Location 1: the lighthouse

**What is visible:** The rotating mechanism is locked facing the hill. An open
book is weighted with a brass key. Iona is checking a nearly empty light reservoir.

**Clue:** A drawing connects the orchard gate, a shared meal, and a full reservoir.
The margin reads, “Promise only the care you can return.”

**Choices:** A: ask Iona what happened; B: inspect the mechanism and book; C: offer
to carry her explanation to the town. Other ideas can work.

**Complication:** A loose shutter bangs in the wind. If unattended it makes the
room noisy; fixing it makes conversation easier. It does not destroy the book.
The Maker might enjoy the task, but anyone can solve it or ask for help.

**What changes:** If the group brings Iona along, she can explain the mechanism
at the gate. If they keep the lighthouse pointed seaward, the orchard path dims
but remains walkable with any ordinary lantern.

## Location 2: the market

**What is visible:** Sef is stopping several neighbors from claiming the new fruit
for themselves. People are excited, confused, and carrying different remembered
versions of the same story.

**Clue:** An older recipe card describes a meal where everyone brought something
small: bread, a song, a repaired stool, a little time. Participation was not a fee.

**Choices:** A: gather what each person remembers; B: help Sef arrange a calm
conversation; C: invite a few willing neighbors to the orchard.

**Complication:** Two neighbors disagree about whether the orchard belongs to the
keeper or to the market. Ask what each is worried about; both fear exclusion.
Let the group propose an arrangement. There is no required “correct speech.”

**What changes:** A clear invitation brings willing helpers. A rushed demand
brings fewer people; those who decline still deserve a voice in future decisions.
No amount of enthusiasm commits absent neighbors to work.

## Location 3: the orchard gate

**What is visible:** The trees glow gently. Pell is setting four mismatched cups
on a long table; adjust the number to the group if it feels welcoming. A sign
says, “A place to rest, a light to share.”

**Clue:** Pell offers the last stored light freely for tonight, then asks what
promise, if any, the town wants to make for the future.

**Choices:** A: renew the shared refuge; B: negotiate a smaller trial; C: release
the agreement and choose another way to maintain the lighthouse. A hybrid is welcome.

**Complication:** Pell initially assumes everyone will agree. A player can point
out that people need to choose their own responsibilities. Pell listens; this is
an opportunity to improve the agreement, not a trap.

**What changes:** The group’s specific choices determine which ending fits. Ask
who is volunteering, what they can realistically offer, and how the arrangement
can be reviewed. Do not assign duties to people who have not agreed.

## Resolve uncertainty without blocking play

First clarify the intended action and any cost the character could reasonably
foresee. If the action is straightforward, it works. For uncertainty, offer two
reasonable outcomes and let the player choose a tradeoff, or use the group’s
agreed game rules. Examples: fix the shutter quickly but need help afterward;
find extra history but spend time; make a bold public proposal and invite questions.
Never attach a fictional penalty to an access need or a player taking a break.

## Endings that honor the decision

**Renew the refuge:** The willing neighbors share a modest meal. The orchard’s
light returns gradually, matching the care people actually offered. Show one
small detail each player contributed.

**Try a smaller promise:** One tree supplies a limited reserve while a few
volunteers try a manageable plan. Sef names a review date within the fiction.
The ending feels hopeful without pretending the practical work is finished.

**Release the old agreement:** Pell gives the last light and thanks the town for
answering honestly. The orchard remains an ordinary public place if the people
choose to care for it. Iona begins planning another lighthouse fuel source.
There is loss, but no moral punishment for declining an inherited obligation.

**An unexpected solution:** Check that it addresses light, access to the orchard,
and consent to any ongoing work. Let NPCs respond to the actual proposal. Preserve
the group’s authorship of the ending.

## Close the table

Ask: “What image stays with your character?” Then offer a brief out-of-character
check-in: one thing to keep, one thing to change, or pass. Confirm any practical
follow-up privately. Do not use the moment to solicit a testimonial or assume
interest in another session.
