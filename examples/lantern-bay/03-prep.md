# Facilitator prep: the minimum useful plan

## Fifteen-minute reading path

Read the truth, NPC motives, three locations, and endings in the adventure. Have
the player introduction available in the group’s preferred form. Choose one way
to track the scene and one way for a player to signal a pause. Nothing else is
required to start a read-through; an actual session still needs the agreed practical
arrangements.

**One-sentence truth:** The keeper turned the lighthouse inward to reveal an
orchard promised to the town; tonight the townspeople must renew or release the
promise by agreeing what the orchard is for.

## Before participants arrive

- Confirm the actual date/platform or venue and a fallback contact method.
- Ask about participation preferences privately, without requesting diagnoses.
- Offer the handout as spoken text, readable HTML, or paper.
- Agree “pause,” “skip,” and “change”; any participant can use them without explanation.
- Check darkness, storms, and tense disagreement as themes; replace them if unwanted.
- Put water, breaks, and the option to step away ahead of dramatic pacing.

## A flexible 90-minute example

| Approximate time | Purpose | Shorter option |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10 | Welcome, preferences, choose roles, opening choice | Read only the opening and role names |
| 10–25 | Lighthouse and first clue | Keeper volunteers the old promise |
| 25–45 | Market or orchard; compare motives | Combine market witnesses at the orchard gate |
| Around midpoint | Offer a break; let the group choose | Break whenever needed |
| 45–70 | Reveal the tradeoff and propose a solution | Present both costs clearly |
| 70–85 | Enact the decision and show consequences | One contribution per willing player |
| 85–90 | Debrief and close | One word or pass from each participant |

The clock is a planning aid, never a test for players. Skip a location or combine
NPCs if energy falls. Do not make the final choice while someone is away unless
they have asked the group to continue.

## If something fails

**Connection fails:** Pause the scene. Use the agreed text/reconnect channel. Do
not add a punishment in the fiction for a technical problem.

**Clue stalls:** State the observable clue directly and offer A/B/C next actions.
The players decide its meaning; finding it does not require a successful roll.

**A theme becomes uncomfortable:** Stop, remove or change it, and resume only
when the participant wants to. No explanation or debate is required.

**The facilitator has limited energy:** Use the two-location path, read NPC motives
as plain statements, and close after the group’s decision. Atmosphere is optional.

## Optional enrichment

Add your own map, ambient sound with independent volume control, or a large-print
clue card only if it helps this group. Record any external asset source and reuse
permission. No licensed game rules or third-party art are included in this pack.
